daily Teams digest. Below is a numbered list of the only facts that
exist for this section today. For each numbered item, write one line of
plain, third-person business prose per distinct point made in that
item's text -- a short, single-point item gets one line; an item that
bundles several separate accomplishments, findings or points together
gets one line per point, never a single line that compresses several
of them into one. A long message is not one fact, it is as many facts
as it actually contains.

A "distinct point" is a separate thing that happened, was found, or was
decided -- not every clause joined by "and" or a comma. A single long
sentence describing one continuous piece of work (e.g. "found the bug,
tracked it to the config loader, and fixed it") is still ONE point and
gets ONE line, even though it has several clauses -- splitting a single
flowing sentence apart at its "and"s produces fragments, not facts.
Split only where the item's own text actually moves on to a separate,
independent thing. A sentence that only qualifies or hedges the point
before it ("though", "not saying we change it, just flagging", "no change
yet") is not a separate thing: put it in that point's line as a short
clause, or leave it out, but do not give it a line of its own.

Rules, none of which are optional:

- Every line must read as a complete, grammatically standalone
  sentence -- never a fragment that only makes sense as a continuation
  of the line before it ("and found a related bug", "turned out it was
  X" with no subject). If you would need the previous line to
  understand a line on its own, it is not yet a complete point: either
  merge it back into that line, or rewrite it with its own subject so
  it stands alone.
- Cover every distinct point in every item. Do not skip an item, do not
  skip a point within an item, and do not invent a point that is not
  present in its text.
- Keep the author's own names, labels, status words and technical terms
  exactly as they wrote them, as one unit. A phrase the author uses to
  name a category or state ("posted but no update", "blocked", "waiting
  on review", a ticket or system name) is one thing: never split it,
  never reorder its words, and never re-attach its words to something
  else in the sentence, because that can reverse what was said ("links
  work on the posted but no update lines" must not become "links work on
  posted lines but not on update lines"). When you are not certain what
  such a phrase refers to, repeat the author's words as written, in
  quotation marks, rather than rephrasing them.
- Base each line only on that item's own message text, given verbatim.
  Never add a name, a reason, an outcome or a detail that is not
  present in that text.
- Every line written for a given item carries that exact item's own
  message_id, character for character -- never invent one, never reuse
  a different item's id, never leave it blank. Multiple lines may (and
  for a multi-point item, should) share the same message_id when they
  come from the same item.
- No adjectives about how good, bad, urgent or important something is.
  State what the message says, not how it should be judged.
- A quote is optional. If you give one, it must be an exact,
  character-for-character substring of that item's own message text --
  not a paraphrase, not a corrected typo, not a shortened version.
